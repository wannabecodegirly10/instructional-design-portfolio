Midpoint Reflection: Design Quality, Ethics & Portfolio Review

I chose to use Backward Design for the revision of this lesson because the model allowed me to think about what I wanted students to walk away with at the end. The Backward Design model begins with the end in mind. Once I determined what I wanted the students to know I was able to develop actionable, non-vague learning objectives. From there, creating formative assessments and activities was easier to align and map to the objectives.

Understanding and incorporating UDL standards is a non-negotiable in learning and instructional design. It is essential for every student to feel as though there are no roadblocks to the lesson objectives, assignments, or assessments.

Peer-review and feedback have been helpful not only with the midterm project, but also through this course and my previous course. It is easy to have blind spots in our work, and I appreciate peer review and feedback because it provides various perspectives on my work. I also find it engaging when I’m able to share my peer-review with my classmates.

AI and ethics initially seem like two components that do not coincide, but with rapidly evolving technology it is important to examine how AI is used, that its use is disclosed, and the way it is used. Ethical considerations are abundant as it relates to AI because it can help us in our personal, professional, and educational lives. Any use of AI in work that is submitted as your own must be disclosed. AI can assist, but never do your work for you.

My next steps are to incorporate additional feedback from peers and instructor to continue to build on the work I have done.

Narrative of Emerging Technology

I chose to analyze Nearpod, an emerging technology tool that can be used to create lessons with additional tools to include interactive activities and assessments.

While I found the platform easy to use and navigate from a UX design, due to the volume of templates and lessons available to choose from, it can be a bit overwhelming for educators and designers who might not know where they want to start with a general design. The platform does meet UDL-Engagement, Representation, and Action & Expression; adherence to those same standards can also act as a barrier when there are too many options.

I enjoyed reviewing, demoing and using Nearpod. They also have a robust Privacy Hub, by their parent company, Renaissance. That hub provides detailed information about how they handle student and institutional data. It also contains their Customer and Data Protection Agreements with various countries.

Assessment Strategies & Data-Informed Design

View PDF Document

Narrative about Data Analytics Dashboard Selection

I selected Airtable for this data analytics dashboard because its granular sharing controls support responsible handling of student data, including the ability to restrict access to users with an lsu.edu email address via SSO. To further protect privacy, I focused on non-sensitive identifiers—Student ID, Completion Status, and Completion Date—and intentionally made Completion Status the primary metric using options such as Completed Assessment 1, Completed Assessment 2, Completed Both, and Extension Granted. The dashboard visualizes these data as percentage completion in a pie chart, complemented by a pivot table that offers a slightly more detailed view, allowing me to monitor assessment progress without exposing unnecessary student information. Alignment to Quality Matters Assessment Standards (QM 5.2) and CETL III Data Privacy & Security guidelines.

Narrative about Interactive Media & Collaborative Selection

I opted to use H5P for my artifact because I thought that interactive activity worked with the type of content I was using, not only for this assignment, but also for what I have built on throughout this term. Multiple-choice and discussion board prompts align with my original Backward Design model for this course. The tie-in with the Padlet discussion board helps foster collaboration because the choices within the H5P activity provide information to reinforce learning without blatantly calling out students as "failing" if they did not select a response directly aligned with one of the study strategies. The discussion board will allow students to directly apply their learning from the overall lesson and the summative checks within the H5P interactive activity, and allows them to share what they learned with their peers and also pick up some additional information from them as well, in an informal way. While the interactive activity is a good fit, that does not mean every student would feel the same way. They might struggle with remaining focused on viewing text-only throughout the activity. I was intentional about the information I used for the activity because I did not want it to be too dense, and therefore defeat the purpose of quick, "bite-sized" pieces of information that can be absorbed or re-visited as needed. This is an activity where I send a short feedback survey. One limitation of this medium is the need to link to another tool to complete the second phase of the activity. My goal was to ensure that part was seamless. Some students might experience issues with the link, or, depending on their device type, the information might render at a different size or cause formatting issues.
